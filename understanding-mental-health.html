Mental Health Tips

Understanding Mental Health

Clear, plain-language information to help you make sense of what you or someone you care about is feeling.

What is mental health?

Mental health includes how we think, feel and act. It affects how we handle stress, relate to others and make choices. Like physical health, it can change over time and sometimes needs care and support.

Common concerns

This is general information only and not a diagnosis.

Anxiety
Ongoing worry or fear that is hard to control and can cause physical symptoms such as a racing heart.
Depression
Lasting low mood, loss of interest and low energy that affect daily life.
Burnout
Deep exhaustion from long-term stress, often linked to work or caring for others.
Grief
A natural response to loss that can bring sadness, anger, numbness or confusion.

Myths and facts

Myth: Mental health problems are a sign of weakness.
Fact: They can affect anyone, and they have nothing to do with strength or character.
Myth: People can just snap out of it.
Fact: Recovery usually takes time, support and sometimes professional care.
Myth: Talking about it makes things worse.
Fact: Talking with someone you trust often brings relief and helps you find support.

When to reach out

Consider talking to a professional if how you feel lasts for weeks, makes daily life harder, or worries you or the people around you. Find a professional or join a support group.
